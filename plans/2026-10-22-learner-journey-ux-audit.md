# Learner-journey UX/UI audit (#194)

Date: 2026-10-22. Method: walked `main` as a first-time English speaker learning Portuguese, on a phone (390×844) and a desktop (1280×900), from an empty browser profile. On every screen we asked: _Where am I? What is my goal? What do I do next? How do I know I'm progressing?_ Trigger: the owner tried to learn Portuguese on the site and got lost quickly.

## Summary

The pieces work, but **there is no path through them**. Lessons, Practice and Progress are three separate tools. Nothing tells a beginner what the goal is (finish A1), what to do today, or how a lesson connects to practice. The core review loop also quizzes beginners on words they have never seen.

## The learner path (proposed spec rows)

| ID  | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| J1  | A learner always sees one goal and one next step: the current level (default A1), an ordered list of its lessons, and a **Continue** button to the next unstudied lesson. Home, Lessons and Progress show the same next step. |
| J2  | Practice draws only from lessons the learner has opened, with a daily cap on new words (default 10). A lesson's "Practise these words" practises that lesson's words. Interleaving (L3) applies across studied lessons.       |
| J3  | Progress leads with the current level's Can-Do statements as a checklist (studied, practised, remembered after a week), with later levels collapsed.                                                                          |

Why these fit the intent: retrieval practice works on material that was encoded first (intent §3, spec L1). Quizzing on never-seen words is guessing, which feels like failing and drives the "don't come back" risk (R3). The Can-Do checklist is the persona Chloe's need (intent §2) and the ability signal S8.

## Sequence

1. Findings 3 and 5 are bugs: fix now (small).
2. J2, then J1, then J3: J2 removes the worst first-session experience; J1 gives the path; J3 shows the goal.
3. Findings 6–10 as one polish pass after J1–J3, because J1–J3 rewrite the same screens.

All of this is goal G1: it has to land before learners are invited at scale, or round one measures confusion, not learning.
